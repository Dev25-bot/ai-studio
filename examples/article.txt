The city council voted on Tuesday to approve a $45 million plan to expand the public transit network, adding three new bus rapid transit lines and extending the light rail service to the northern suburbs. Supporters argued that the expansion would reduce traffic congestion, cut carbon emissions and give lower-income residents better access to jobs in the downtown core. Opponents raised concerns about the cost, noting that the plan relies on a temporary sales tax increase of half a percent that will remain in place for eight years. Construction on the first bus line is expected to begin next spring, with the full network scheduled for completion by 2031. Transit officials said ridership on existing routes has grown 18 percent over the past two years, and that several lines are already operating at capacity during peak hours. The mayor called the vote "a turning point for how our city moves," while a coalition of business owners along the proposed light rail route asked the council to provide compensation for disruption during construction. The council agreed to hold a series of public meetings this fall to gather input on station locations and to publish quarterly progress reports.
